# VAJRA model, repository and data-access decisions

Verified on 30 September 2026. This note answers the WeatherNext 3, TimesFM 3.0 and Jev questions against official publications, source repositories and provider documentation. Recommendations describe the next implementation stage. They do not imply that these models have been installed, trained or validated in VAJRA.

The decision is to keep a compact, sensor-specific nowcasting system. Use established readers, radar motion baselines and a directly trained lightning predictor. Test WeatherNext 3 as optional environmental context after access and causal historical evaluation are possible. TimesFM is a secondary experiment for numerical storm or station time series. Neither Jev nor a general language model is necessary to calculate weather probabilities.

## 1. The named releases are real, but have different jobs

| Name | Verified status | VAJRA decision |
|---|---|---|
| WeatherNext 3 | Google announced it on 3 September 2026. The developer guide calls the model release August 2026. Its technical report is a September 2026 arXiv preprint. | Conditional use of forecast outputs as background information. Do not replace the local lightning model. |
| TimesFM 3.0 | Official Google Research repository announces an August 2026 release and links official weights. It is a numerical time-series model. | Optional research comparator. Its default weights cannot be used in production under the current published licence. |
| TypeSafe Jev | Official documentation describes a hosted, text-only decision model. Public SDK source is available. | Not needed for weather inference. A later text-workflow experiment needs its own evaluation. |

Sources: [WeatherNext announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/), [WeatherNext specifications](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/models), [TimesFM repository](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm), [Jev model documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models).

### WeatherNext 3: what it actually predicts

It is a global probabilistic weather model. The paper describes an encode-process-decode mesh transformer using Functional Generative Networks. Separate encoders and decoders handle different input grids; sampled noise produces ensemble members. Inputs include ECMWF analysis/short forecasts and an hourly, 11-channel geostationary satellite mosaic. Training includes reanalysis, observed station temperature/dewpoint, satellite-derived precipitation and cyclone records. This is numerical weather modelling, not an LLM reading weather descriptions. [Rasp et al., *WeatherNext 3: Increasing resolution and performance of global weather models with raw observations*, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03582).

The operational specification distinguishes these products:

| Property | Published specification |
|---|---|
| Update and ensemble | Hourly initializations; 64 members |
| Forecast duration | 15 days for 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC cycles; 48 hours for interim hourly runs |
| Finest output | Approximately 5 km, station-trained 2 m temperature and dewpoint |
| Surface grid | Approximately 10 km, including wind, clouds, radiation and hourly precipitation |
| Upper air | Approximately 25 km, with pressure-level products in the main six-hourly cycles |
| Historical service | 2026 available; 2024 and 2025 described as being backfilled |

These are variable-specific resolutions. "5 km weather model" does not mean a 5 km precipitation or lightning forecast. One-hour precipitation accumulation must also remain distinct from instantaneous rain rate. [Official model and variable guide](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/models).

Publication time is especially relevant to our 30-minute task. Google's schedule targets the 00 UTC main run for 07:45 UTC in Cloud Storage and 08:10 UTC in BigQuery/Earth Engine. Interim runs target initialization plus 7 hours 10 minutes in Cloud Storage. These are delivery estimates, not guarantees. [Dissemination schedule](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/dissemination).

This does not mean all inputs are seven hours old. The paper uses satellite observations newer than the nominal analysis initialization. For example, it describes a 14 UTC decision with satellite images through 13 UTC and older HRES context. Consequently, storing only `initialization_time` gives a misleading picture of freshness. Preserve the nominal initialization, latest input observation time where supplied, provider publication time, local receipt time and forecast valid time separately. The latter is our proposed ingest rule. [Paper, sections 2.3 and 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03582v1).

Access and weights require another distinction. The official open-source page explicitly says WeatherNext 3 is not open source. The public WeatherNext repository contains WeatherNext 2 and earlier GraphCast/GenCast work. It is not a downloadable WN3 checkpoint. [Google open-source status](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/osmodel).

Forecast access is offered through Cloud Storage, Earth Engine and BigQuery. The guide requires an access request and account allowlisting for real-time operational datasets; it quotes a typical review time of 5 to 7 business days. Cloud Storage provides the full ensemble, while the other listed interfaces expose summary statistics. Historical data has CC BY 4.0 terms; real-time/future data has separate experimental terms. The time distinction refers to the time the data relates to, not simply how long a file has sat in our cache. Infrastructure charges and provider terms remain separate from model availability. [Access guide and linked terms](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/access-forecast).

**Use for VAJRA:** compare a compact model with and without available WeatherNext environmental fields on the same held-out Indian storms. Global coverage includes India geographically, but no reviewed source establishes our exact first-lightning target, ground-network labels or local calibration. The published variable list contains no lightning output. Grid interpolation cannot create new convective detail. Keep the dependency optional and preserve a validated no-WeatherNext path. Requesting access, downloading its forecasts and measuring performance remain future work.

### TimesFM 3.0: useful numerical forecasting, with a production restriction

The correct model name is **TimesFM**, meaning Time Series Foundation Model. The official repository now documents 3.0, including native multivariate forecasts and past-only or past-and-future covariates. Guidance for 2.5, such as its XReg interface, should not be presented as the 3.0 interface. The repository identifies 2.5 as the September 2025 predecessor. [Official release and examples](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm).

Google's 3.0 model card describes a stacked mixing transformer with variate attention and provides PyTorch weights. Its citation still points to the original decoder-only TimesFM paper. That original paper is not evidence for every new 3.0 capability or a separate peer-reviewed 3.0 publication. [Official model card](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch).

The repository code is Apache-2.0; weights through 2.5 retain Apache-2.0. The 3.0 checkpoint instead uses `timesfm-non-commercial-license-v1.0`, which restricts use to non-commercial, non-production purposes. A free government warning service is still a production service; "we do not charge users" does not remove the production restriction stated in the licence. Keep 3.0 out of the deployment plan unless appropriate rights are obtained. [Checkpoint licence](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch/blob/main/LICENSE), [repository licence notice](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm).

**Possible experiment:** forecast a tracked storm's area, cloud-top temperature or echo-intensity summary, then compare with persistence, an autoregressive model and a small recurrent model. This evaluates an intermediate numerical forecast. It does not turn TimesFM into an image reader or validate lightning probabilities. Feeding each image pixel as an unrelated time series discards explicit geometry and transport; treating all pixels as variates does not establish effective spatial modelling either. Future covariates must already be available at issue time, such as a forecast issued earlier. Future observed lightning or reanalysis values are not permissible covariates in a causal replay.

### Jev and language models: optional explanation tools

TypeSafe documents Jev 1.13 as a text/JSON-state decision model behind `POST /v1/systemone`. It accepts no direct image, audio or video input, and the vendor does not offer customer fine-tuning through the documented model interface. The Python and JavaScript SDKs are public MIT-licensed clients. Installing them does not download the hosted model. No official Jev weight release or self-hosted checkpoint was identified in the vendor's model documentation or public organization reviewed here. Independently named Jev-like models must not be described as official Jev. [Model documentation](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models), [official Python SDK](https://github.com/typesafe-ai/typesafe-sdk-python), [official JavaScript SDK](https://github.com/typesafe-ai/typesafe-sdk-js).

For this project, fixed rules can select a warning template from verified probabilities, data status and an officer's decision. A future LLM could draft an explanation from a structured forecast receipt, with quantities checked against that receipt before display. Jev could classify an incoming text report after task-specific testing. Neither should infer a lightning probability from prose, replace numerical verification, silently change a forecast threshold or sit on the required delivery path. A language model's confidence is not a meteorological probability.

## 2. Recommended algorithm at each stage

The following is the proposed regional system. The current app's radar image processing and synthetic fusion demonstration do not implement this entire stack.

| Stage | Recommended algorithm and implementation | Required evidence and failure handling |
|---|---|---|
| Radar decoding and geometry | Py-ART for suitable numeric volumes; wradlib for georeferencing, clutter/attenuation tools and relevant product conversions. Select the reader against the actual provider sample. | Confirm units, projection, calibration, scan height and quality flags. Missing coverage and no echo are separate masks. Never derive a physical volume from a coloured website image. |
| Radar motion | Persistence baseline, then pysteps Lucas-Kanade motion and semi-Lagrangian extrapolation. Compare with the existing global-translation and Horn-Schunck experiments. | Score on common valid support and inspect displacement. Motion can fail with weak echoes, fast growth or mixed motions. Retain explicit invalid pixels. |
| Growth and decay | LINDA as the established motion-plus-development comparator; then a small learned residual network if matched data justify it. | LINDA is a precipitation method. Declare whether the target is rain rate, reflectivity or another measurement, and do not silently reuse incompatible units. A sharper field is not proof of better probability forecasts. |
| Storm objects | DATing detection/association, recording birth, continuation, merge and split events through available observations. Keep a dense background map alongside tracks. | Future frames must not revise features used in an earlier forecast. Object thresholds affect lineage. A tracked radar object is not a guaranteed physical storm identity. |
| Satellite | Satpy decode/calibration and geolocation; preserve channel-specific masks and acquisition times. Start with temperature, cooling trend and local spatial features; then a small U-Net or temporal convolutional/recurrent encoder. | INSAT and ABI/AHI channels are different instruments. Verify readers against actual files, day/night behavior, viewing geometry and parallax. Cold clouds are predictors, not lightning labels. |
| Lightning observations and target | Parse the provider's actual event hierarchy; deduplicate; retain detection/coverage status. Train a binary next-30-minute, within-8-km target and evaluate initiation separately. | Decide total lightning versus cloud-to-ground before training. A first recorded detection is not necessarily the first physical flash. Unknown coverage creates unknown/censored labels, not negative examples. |
| NWP context | xarray with a GRIB decoder, using the latest available GFS/IFS cycle and valid forecast fields. Begin with moisture, CAPE/CIN and wind context; obtain vertical profiles before deriving shear. | Preserve initialization, lead, accumulation interval and publication/receipt times. Reanalysis is retrospective, not an operational forecast. Resampling a coarse field does not make its effective information 2 km. |
| Fusion | Begin with a transparent logistic/boosted-tree feature baseline; then test separate compact radar/satellite encoders, temporal ConvGRU features and explicit source-age/validity inputs, plus NWP/static context. | Evaluate radar-only, satellite-only and combined models on identical storms. Test delayed and absent sensors. Missingness must not mechanically reduce hazard probability. A model can abstain when its validated input conditions fail. |
| First-event timing | Conditional discrete-time hazard head, initially six 5-minute bins, only after adequate quiet-history and follow-up coverage exist. | Cumulative probabilities come from conditional hazards, not independent sigmoid outputs. Retain the simpler 30-minute classifier as a comparator. Coverage-related censoring may be informative. |
| Verification and delivery | Separate calibration set; Brier/reliability and precision-recall for lightning, first-flash misses and false-alert duration, plus spatial radar scores. A versioned rule turns probabilities into a reviewable officer action. | Separate storms/days/geography across splits. Fit preprocessing only on usable training data and calibration only on its designated partition, distinct from checkpoint-selection validation and final test. Operator benefit needs its own prospective evaluation. |

Implementation evidence: [pysteps capabilities](https://github.com/pySTEPS/pysteps), [LINDA API](https://pysteps.readthedocs.io/en/stable/generated/pysteps.nowcasts.linda.forecast.html), [DATing API](https://pysteps.readthedocs.io/en/stable/generated/pysteps.tracking.tdating.dating.html), [Satpy INSAT-3D reader](https://satpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/satpy.readers.insat3d_img_l1b_h5.html).

The scientific precedent is already strong. LightningCast predicts GLM-observed lightning using satellite imagery. Its 2026 radar study evaluates added reflectivity and first-flash cases. Leinonen and colleagues combine radar, satellite, lightning, NWP and terrain for hazard nowcasting. These support a locally validated adaptation; they rule out claiming that multisensor fusion or first-lightning evaluation is itself new. [LightningCast, 2022](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/52868), [radar-LightningCast, 2026](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/75509), [Leinonen et al., 2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022GL101626). Censoring, proper scores and spatial verification references are in the [curated paper library](PAPER_REFERENCE_LIBRARY.md).

The local collection manifests already provide concrete download evidence: [India](../data/government/india/manifest.json), [NOAA](../data/government/noaa/manifest.json) and [GFS](../data/government/gfs/manifest.json). These are small, partly unmatched starter samples. They do not collectively form a paired Indian storm dataset. The existing NOAA fixture contains one ABI channel; that is insufficient for the four-channel LightningCast interface. The saved GFS subset contains surface winds; vertical wind shear requires additional levels.

## 5. Build order and promotion criteria

1. **Preserve the working radar experiment.** Add tested pysteps baselines to a larger numeric radar corpus. Keep source masks and measurement units explicit. Measure loading, preparation, inference and serialization together on the deployment machine.
2. **Make one paired lightning experiment reproducible.** Start with a documented US multimodal benchmark if Indian matched data remains unavailable. Freeze channels, target, label coverage, storm-level splits and data hashes before model tuning.
3. **Train a small lightning model first.** Compare climatology, persistence, satellite-only, radar-only and combined features. Use a compact temporal spatial model only when it beats the transparent baseline on held-out storms. Evaluate first-event cases separately from already-electrified storms.
4. **Add Indian data through actual product agreements.** Confirm enough coincident observations and lightning coverage before promising a 2-km grid, ten-minute cadence or full-region service. Keep unknown labels unknown and preserve every provider's rights and provenance.
5. **Evaluate optional providers and larger models one at a time.** WeatherNext context, TimesFM summaries, Earthformer or generative rainfall models each need an ablation against the current best baseline. Record latency, memory, calibration, missed events and false-alert duration. A new model name is not a release criterion.

The planned input shape remains six ten-minute observations and a 128 by 128 central grid at 2 km, with a separately specified spatial halo. That is a proposed compute/data contract, not a claim that our current samples meet it. A 30-minute event target also needs complete subsequent coverage, plus the declared quiet-history window for initiation. Build time alignment and label isolation before training the proposed fusion network.

The defensible differentiation is a measured Indian pilot result: useful local warning lead time, reliable probabilities and clear operator decisions under realistic missing or delayed observations. The required comparison is against established methods and official operational guidance where accessible. None of the reviewed releases removes that experiment.
