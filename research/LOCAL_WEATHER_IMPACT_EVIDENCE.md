# Indian local weather warnings: evidence, gaps and measurable benefits

Research cut-off and source access date: **30 September 2026**. This note extends [PROBLEM_EVIDENCE.md](PROBLEM_EVIDENCE.md), [SURVEYS_AND_NEWS_2026.md](SURVEYS_AND_NEWS_2026.md) and [VIABILITY_AND_SUSTAINABILITY_EVIDENCE.md](VIABILITY_AND_SUSTAINABILITY_EVIDENCE.md). It does not report a new VAJRA field survey or an evaluation of VAJRA. The accompanying [CSV](LOCAL_WEATHER_IMPACT_EVIDENCE.csv) records the scope and limitations of each evidence item.

The defensible problem is uneven decision support across locations, lead times and users. Existing Indian forecasts and warning services have substantial capabilities. A useful project must show an improvement for a defined decision and population, rather than infer forecast failure from disaster losses or government expenditure.

## Evidence register

The register contains 14 distinct primary-source groups. Mirrors of a report and parliamentary replies reproducing the same underlying records are cross-checks, not independent studies. Publication dates and observation periods are deliberately separate.

### E01. Official forecast skill is specific to the product and lead time

MoES's **11 March 2026** parliamentary reply reports March–June thunderstorm nowcast probability of detection, POD, of **0.92 in 2025 versus 0.83 in 2022**, and **0.89 for 24-hour forecasts in 2025**. Its heatwave example gives 2025 POD of **98%, 75% and 46%** at one-, three- and five-day leads. The reply describes products at district, station, block and larger scales, but supplies no thunderstorm event counts or local verification table. [Official reply, pp. 1–3](https://www.moes.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/03/9fbc5fe044e9f667b2569306b65d3181.pdf).

POD measures detected observed events. It does not measure false alarms, probability calibration, warning receipt or village-specific skill. The reply also states that no recent direct public-benefit assessment was undertaken. Its monetary benefit assertion is therefore not adopted as a project ROI estimate. Source strength: official performance summary, with incomplete verification denominators.

### E02. A local Indian verification study uses actual panchayat observations

Singh, Mall, Singh and Das, published **2 January 2024**, evaluated IMD's **3 km WRF** daily rainfall forecasts against **812 panchayat gauges** during **June–September 2020 and 2021**: Supaul 175, East Champaran 395 and Nalanda 242, Bihar. Forecasts extended to three days. The paper reports approximately **70% correct rain/no-rain classification**, particularly in Supaul, and false-alarm ratio below 0.3 in 90% of panchayats. [MAUSAM article and DOI 10.54302/mausam.v75i1.6037](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/6037), [original PDF, pp. 197–204](https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/download/6037/5689).

The 812 sites are not 812 independent storms. Daily rainfall scores do not establish five-minute lightning skill, and performance differs by district, month, threshold and lead. This peer-reviewed study supports local verification; it does not validate VAJRA or guarantee that its gauge records are publicly downloadable.

### E03. Intermediate users identified communication barriers

The **January 2022** IMD monograph by Bhan, Singh, Vishnoi and Yadubhushanam surveyed **515 intermediate users, 371 government and 144 nongovernment**, through an online form circulated via IMD offices between **5 January and 28 February 2021**. Reported response barriers included inadequate uncertainty communication **50%**, insufficient awareness of safety actions **43%**, and missing impact information **40%**. [Author-uploaded primary monograph, methods p. 8 and Figure 13 pp. 19–20](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359699450_Expectation_and_Utilization_Behaviour_of_the_IntermediateUsers_of_Weather_and_Climate_Services_in_India).

This is a technical report and a selected professional sample, not a national household survey. The figure does not disclose item-valid denominators, so rounded percentages must not become exact respondent counts. Historical perceptions do not prove current app failure. They motivate testing whether users understand uncertainty and recommended actions.

### E04. Existing advisory users report changing agricultural decisions

The **July 2020 NCAER** evaluation commissioned by MoES reports a **2019 face-to-face beneficiary survey of 6,098 people**: 3,965 farmers, 1,376 livestock owners and 757 marine fishermen. Farmer respondents covered **121 districts in 11 states**. **98% of surveyed farmers** reported changing at least one of nine agricultural practices using advisories. [Original IMD-hosted report, printed pp. 15–21, PDF pp. 39–45](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/uploads/survey/NCAER2020.pdf).

Sampling selected engaged beneficiaries from an existing service database, including sustained voice-message listeners who sought clarification. This is evidence that advisories inform decisions among those users. Selection, recall and self-report prevent treating it as national adoption or causal income improvement. Its separate IVRS exercise has a different denominator. Published programme-level economic estimates must not be transferred to VAJRA.

### E05. A randomized Indian experiment demonstrates behavior change, within a narrow scope

Burlig, Jina, Kelley, Lane and Sahai's **May 2025 working-paper version** studies **1,240 households in 250 Telangana villages during 2022**. The forecast, insurance and comparison arms comprised **497, 248 and 495 households**, respectively. The intervention supplied a monsoon-onset forecast approximately **40 days ahead**. Random assignment supports causal interpretation of the intervention's effects on beliefs and investment decisions. Effects depended on farmers' prior expectations; subsequent unexpected flooding complicates a simple forecast-to-profit account. [Original research paper](https://www.atai-research.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/20250528_BJKLS_forecasts.pdf), [first-party J-PAL study record and trial registration link](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-long-range-weather-forecasts-farmers-india).

This is a working paper, not claimed here as a completed peer-reviewed publication. Long-range sowing decisions differ from short-lead thunderstorm decisions. No numerical welfare or income estimate is imported across paper versions, regions or into VAJRA.

### E06. Household connectivity does not ensure individual warning access

NSO's **29 May 2025** release covers the **January–March 2025 Comprehensive Modular Survey: Telecom**, a national stratified survey of **34,950 households and 142,065 people**, excluding specified inaccessible Andaman and Nicobar villages. **82.1% of rural households** reported a smartphone. Among **rural people aged 15 or older**, personal mobile ownership was **80.7% for men and 48.4% for women**. These are different denominators. [Official press PDF, sample p. 2 and tables pp. 4–5](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/may/doc2025529560001.pdf), [MoSPI copy](https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Final_press%20release_CMS_T.pdf).

The survey measures reported ICT access and use, not weather-warning receipt or comprehension. A household handset may be unavailable to an exposed worker. These results justify testing shared-device and assisted-access flows; they cannot quantify VAJRA's reachable audience or paid market.

### E07. A small peer-reviewed advisory comparison illustrates both possible value and confounding

Dupdal and colleagues' **2020** study, published online **7 November**, compared **40 advisory users in Puthenchira with 40 nonusers in Vellangallur**, Mukundapuram taluka, Thrissur, Kerala. The paper reports user incomes **19–34% higher** than nonusers and **55% of the 40 users** rating advisories very good. [ICAR journal record](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJEE/article/view/106999), [original paper, pp. 10–16](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJEE/article/download/106999/42147).

The inspected methods do not specify the field-survey year. Farmers were sampled, but advisory access was not randomly assigned; the two villages and their farmer profiles differ. The income comparison is observational, not the causal return to adopting an app. Its willingness-to-pay model does not establish present demand or pricing for VAJRA.

### E08. Lightning deaths are a substantial, historically verified burden

NCRB figures reproduced in **EnviStats India 2024**, Statement 4.08, record **2,887 lightning deaths out of 8,060 deaths attributed to forces of nature in 2022**. The calculated share is **35.82%**. For 2021 the corresponding figures are **2,880/7,126, or 40.42%**. [MoSPI original, printed p. 200, PDF p. 215](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/reports_and_publication/statistical_publication/EnviStats/Complete_ES1_2024.pdf).

The same table's **2018–2020 totals, 2,357, 2,876 and 2,862**, agree with a separate **1 December 2021** parliamentary publication. [MoES/PIB, Annexure I](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1776751&lang=2&reg=48). This checks transcription across publications sharing NCRB records, not independent mortality measurement. These historical administrative counts do not identify missed warnings, preventable deaths, or VAJRA's impact. They are not presented as the latest annual totals.

### E09. Crop area affected is an exposure measure, not a monetary loss estimate

The Agriculture Ministry's **7 February 2025** reply reports **14.24 lakh hectares of cropped area affected in FY2024–25 as of 27 January 2025**, based on state/UT reports of hydro-meteorological disasters. Its 14 nonmissing state/UT values sum to the published total. The reply separately reports **₹1,72,138 crore** of cumulative PMFBY/RWBCIS claims benefiting **19.59 crore farmer applications since 2016**. [Official reply and annexure](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2100762&lang=2&reg=48).

Affected area is neither area completely destroyed nor lost output or currency value. Insurance claims have a different period, scope and mechanism; applications are not unique farmers. Neither statistic measures loss caused by forecast errors. Dashes in the annexure are missing reports, not demonstrated zero damage.

### E10. A later official crop table fails a basic arithmetic check

The Home Ministry's **4 August 2026** reply, *Financial assistance for natural disasters*, labels Annexure I provisional. For **FY2024–25**, the published cropped-area total is **13.11 lakh hectares**, but its 14 nonmissing state/UT rows sum to **13.20**. The discrepancy was confirmed by reading the table and independently parsing the live HTML with decimal arithmetic. [Official annexure](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2294241&lang=1&reg=1).

Neither value is silently substituted for the other. E09 remains a valid dated earlier snapshot, not a final-year estimate. Differences between reporting dates may reflect revisions, but that does not explain an internally inconsistent total. The newer FY2025–26 table is not promoted to a headline loss estimate while reporting completeness and underlying records remain unresolved.

### E11. Mission Mausam demonstrates public investment and existing technical ambition

Cabinet approved **₹2,000 crore for two years** on **11 September 2024**. A **13 August 2026** MoES parliamentary reply identifies the original period as **FY2024–25 and FY2025–26** and reports a **FY2026–27 Budget Estimate of ₹1,342.29 crore**, comprising ₹855.29 crore revenue and ₹487 crore capital. [Original Cabinet approval](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2053898&lang=2&reg=48), [later official budget reply](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2298819&lang=1&reg=3).

Programme approval and a Budget Estimate are not audited spending or a procurement opportunity for this project. Existing plans include better observations, modelling, AI/ML and decision support. Alignment with those objectives is credible; claiming these ideas are unprecedented is not. Do not add other announced investments without checking accounting overlap.

### E12. HPC funding is infrastructure context

MoES's **27 September 2024** announcement describes **₹850 crore** invested in the Arka/Arunika weather and climate computing project at **IITM Pune and NCMRWF Noida**. [Official inauguration announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2059397&lang=2&reg=48).

This is an announced infrastructure investment, not a project-level benefit evaluation, audited VAJRA-comparable cost, or evidence that operational forecasting failed. It covers capabilities with much broader scope than a local nowcasting application. The amount must not be added to Mission Mausam totals as if the sources establish disjoint expenditures.

### E13. Multilingual, targeted official emergency alerts already exist

The Department of Telecommunications' **2 May 2026** launch announcement describes a cell-broadcast public-warning service integrated with **NDMA SACHET**, with tower/cluster targeting, multilingual alerts and read-aloud support on supported handsets. [Official launch release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2257499&lang=1&reg=3).

This is a capability announcement, not a representative test of delivery, comprehension or protective action. Read-aloud availability is device-dependent. VAJRA should complement official alerts and verify local usability. It cannot claim that geotargeting, multilingual delivery or speech are new inventions, or assume authorization to originate official cell broadcasts.

### E14. Government specialists explicitly identified the last-mile problem

A **28 June 2021** MoES workshop report, involving meteorological institutions, media and disaster managers, identified problems in communicating and acting on warnings despite improved monitoring and prediction. It emphasized local communication, community participation and coordination. [Official workshop report](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1730978&lang=2&reg=48).

This is institutional problem identification, not a survey with a population denominator. It establishes a reason to investigate the delivery and action chain. It does not quantify present-day failure, especially after subsequent official service expansion.

## What these sources support

The inference is that VAJRA needs separate evidence for forecast quality, delivery and user action. None can substitute for another. E01–E02 establish why verification must retain hazard, location, season, lead time and event definition. E03–E07 motivate user research and decision-specific evaluation. E08–E10 establish reported harm and the need to inspect reporting quality. E11–E14 identify the existing public system with which a useful service must work.

| Proposed improvement | Evidence that motivates it | How to test it without claiming an unmeasured benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Show validity interval, observation age and data gaps beside every local result | Forecast products have different scales; local skill must be verified | Inject late/missing inputs; measure stale results presented as current, and recovery time |
| Calibrate probabilities for a defined hazard and horizon | Detection scores do not establish probability reliability | Fit calibration only on a designated calibration partition; assess untouched events using Brier score, reliability bins with counts and intervals, and event-level uncertainty |
| Publish local and seasonal results alongside pooled results | Bihar point verification and lead-specific official scores | Compare event-grouped held-out districts/seasons; show sample size and support rather than force unstable subgroup estimates |
| Give short, comprehensible actions in a user's language | Intermediate-user barriers and unequal personal phone access | Conduct consented task tests, including shared-device users; record language, comprehension denominator, response time and errors |
| Offer a trusted intermediary workflow | Household device access does not establish individual receipt | Measure reached users against a prespecified exposed-user roster; distinguish sent, delivered, opened, acknowledged and understood |
| Support specific agricultural decisions | Advisory surveys and the Telangana experiment | Select one crop, decision and forecast horizon with an agronomist; record actual costs and outcomes rather than import another study's income percentage |
| Minimize unnecessary interruptions while preserving useful lead time | POD alone omits false alarms | Report misses, false-alarm ratio, precision, recall, CSI and first useful warning lead time for the same event definition |

These are proposed evaluation methods. They are not claims that the implementation already achieves the outcomes.

## A credible benefit evaluation

Start with a bounded partner pilot and preregister its questions. Define a place, exposed population, hazard threshold, forecast horizon and operational decision. Continue official warning access for all participants. First test comprehension and drill behavior; independently review any later comparison of added advisory support. An app-open or acknowledgement event must never be counted as a person sheltered.

For agricultural value, compare documented decision costs and outcomes with an appropriate counterfactual across enough events and seasons. Record confounders such as crop, irrigation, prior advisory access and exposure. For life safety, deaths are rare relative to ordinary user actions and are influenced by exposure, housing, shelter access and response. A small demonstration cannot establish mortality reduction. Report measured intermediate outcomes and leave lives saved unknown.

Operational efficiency also needs direct measurement: compute time per forecast, memory, data transferred, failed ingestion rate, unavailable-observation duration, energy where metering exists, and cost per monitored area or active partner. A faster model has not shown a public benefit until the downstream decision improves.

## Verification record and unresolved evidence

| Check | Result and scope |
|---|---|
| Official mortality table read through direct PDF retrieval and text extraction | The 2018–2020 series agrees with a separate official parliamentary table; 2021 and 2022 percentages were recomputed from the stated forces-of-nature denominator |
| Telecom survey method and table checked in official MoSPI/PIB versions | National sample size, household smartphone rate and personal mobile ownership have distinct denominators; mirrors are the same survey |
| Mission approval compared with the later parliamentary reply | Initial approval agrees; later allocation is a Budget Estimate, not expenditure |
| Both crop annexures inspected; newer HTML parsed separately | Earlier 14.24 lakh ha snapshot reconciles; newer FY2024–25 printed total differs from its rows by 0.09 lakh ha |
| Peer-reviewed Bihar and Thrissur PDFs retrieved directly | Methods and actual study geographies checked; Thrissur field year remains unspecified in inspected methods |
| NCAER original PDF read beyond the abstract | Engaged-beneficiary sampling is a material limitation; face-to-face and IVRS populations are separate |
| Telangana original paper checked against the first-party evaluation record | Same trial; working-paper versions differ, so no pooled numerical welfare estimate is reported |

All cited source groups were inspected through their primary page, full report, or both. Some hosts intermittently time out in the browser tool while direct HTTPS succeeds; retrieval did not disable TLS verification or use credentials. There is no claim that the project's repository stores every source document.

The official ADSI 2023 catalogue was located, but its data endpoint did not yield an inspectable table in this research session. Secondary 2023/2024 lightning totals are excluded; the older verified series is explicitly historical. The IMD 2024 monsoon report's indexed monthly lightning discussion was found, but direct access returned HTTP 403, so it is not counted among the 14 source groups or used for numeric spatial claims. A 2026 adoption paper available only as a search snippet is likewise excluded.

The newspaper items in [SURVEYS_AND_NEWS_2026.md](SURVEYS_AND_NEWS_2026.md) remain contextual reporting. They do not replace the original studies or official event records, and provisional combined storm fatalities must not be relabelled as lightning-only deaths. No current representative national survey of weather-warning comprehension was recovered. That is an evidence gap to address with a properly designed pilot, not permission to invent a failure rate.

## Claims to keep out of the proposal

- Government spent a specified amount and people still died, therefore forecasts failed. Spending, exposure and mortality do not identify that causal chain.
- A 0.92 POD means 92% of all predictions are correct, 92% probability of a local event, or 92% of people are protected.
- Household smartphone ownership equals individual alert reach.
- All affected crop area was destroyed, or insurance payouts equal all economic weather loss.
- A selected beneficiary survey measures national adoption or willingness to buy VAJRA.
- A 40-day monsoon forecast experiment proves the benefit of a 30-minute lightning alert.
- The prototype saves a stated number of lives or rupees, beats IMD, or is validated for Indian lightning without a matching prospective evaluation.
